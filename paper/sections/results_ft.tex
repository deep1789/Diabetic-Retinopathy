\subsection{Comparison with an end-to-end fine-tuned CNN}
\label{sec:res-ft}
To place the frozen-embedding results in context, we fine-tuned a ResNet-18 end-to-end on the 6260 gradable training images (Gaussian-filtered, $224\times224$ crops from $256\times256$, ordinal cumulative-link loss with the same soft targets, AdamW, 8 epochs, one run; about 17 minutes of CPU time). This is a deliberately modest reference rather than a tuned state-of-the-art model: it has a small backbone, low resolution, a short schedule, one seed, and it does not use lesion information.

Table~\ref{tab:ft} shows that the fine-tuned network reaches a QWK of \nFTQWK, an accuracy of \nFTAcc\% and an AUC for referable DR of \nFTAUC, with a low calibration error (\nFTECE). It is clearly better than the frozen-embedding models that do not use lesion evidence (B2: 0.638; paired difference \nFTdBtwoMean\ for B2 minus fine-tuned, interval [\nFTdBtwoLo, \nFTdBtwoHi]), which confirms that fine-tuning on DR images adds information that a frozen ImageNet embedding lacks. At the same time, \mname\ remains better than this reference (\nMainQWK\ versus \nFTQWK; paired difference \nFTdMainMean, 95\% interval [\nFTdMainLo, \nFTdMainHi]), although it only uses a frozen backbone, so lesion evidence derived from 383 pixel-annotated images is worth more than end-to-end fine-tuning of a small backbone on 6260 image-level labels in this setting. We do not draw conclusions about larger or longer-trained end-to-end models: a fine-tuned high-resolution network, a stronger backbone or a retinal foundation model would very probably exceed both, and combining such a backbone with lesion evidence (the natural extension of this work) was beyond our compute. The fine-tuned model shows the same optimism on the validation partition as the other models (validation QWK 0.858 versus 0.700 on the test set), which is consistent with the partition shift documented in Section~\ref{sec:res-conf}.
