\textbf{Background.} Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is graded from lesions, yet grading, lesion segmentation and lesion detection are usually solved by separate models, and grades are not required to agree with lesion evidence.
\textbf{Objective.} We test whether grounding the grade in lesion evidence from a segmentation network, with ordinal soft targets, a soft-logic clinical-rule loss and conformal referral sets, improves grading, and we report segmentation and detection on the same data.
\textbf{Methods.} On DDR (\num{13673} fundus images, 757 with lesion masks and boxes), a U-Net yields four lesion maps that a differentiable bottleneck turns into 24 evidence tokens; a small transformer combines them with a frozen image embedding. All experiments ran on a four-core CPU; a leak-controlled test set (3633 images) was used because the lesion and grading partitions overlap.
\textbf{Results.} Lesion evidence raised the quadratic-weighted kappa from \nBoneQWK--\nBtwoQWK\ (embedding only) to \nMainQWK\ (95\% CI \nMainCIlo--\nMainCIhi; AUC for referable DR \nMainAUC), above a fine-tuned ResNet-18 reference (\nFTQWK). Whole-field statistics carried most of this gain; zone resolution and the rule loss added nothing measurable, whereas soft targets lowered calibration error to \nMainECE. Segmentation reached a mean AUPR of \nSegAUPRFtlbg\ (microaneurysms $\le0.17$) and detection a mean AP$_{0.3}$ of \nDetMapFtlbg. Conformal sets reached nominal coverage under exchangeability but not across the official validation and test partitions, which differ in difficulty; zero-shot transfer to a $224\times224$ external set failed.
\textbf{Conclusions.} Lesion-derived evidence is a cheap, clear source of grading information; other components were modest, and generalisation remains open.
